The short version · updated 16 November 2026

Run your bank through Prague’s pneumatic post.

Choose worthwhile business tasks, send requests across one shared map, and bring the replies home. Earn money from completed business while paying for the correspondence.

2–9 players · target40–50 minutes · targetIndividual competition1936/37 · working setting

A developing concept. The overview below separates our decisions from ideas still to test.

What we have decided

  • Everyone represents a bank. Players use one shared Prague map. The exact historical bank roster is still open.
  • Success is measured in Kč. Completed business earns income; postal expenses reduce what you keep.
  • You choose from a personal selection of specific business tasks. New offers arrive each morning. Unaccepted offers remain available until their shown expiry; exact lifetimes and offer counts remain open.
  • Plan the whole day together. Everyone chooses all actions for one game day simultaneously in secret. Everyone reveals their complete plan at once, then plans play out publicly. Accepted tasks are visible. Players also choose the order of their actions. Conflict-resolution rules remain open.
  • Timing and credits limit your workload. Capsules have no letter-count limit in the game. Credits pay for handling business tasks, with more demanding cases requiring more effort; money pays postage. Batching letters saves postage without removing the handling cost of each task. Pay a task’s full handling cost upfront when starting it; reply processing is covered. Everyone starts each morning with the standard credit budget; unused credits do not accumulate. Exact amounts remain open.
  • Several short working days. Each day has a new simultaneous secret planning phase, a full reveal and public resolution. The number of days is undecided.
  • Cases continue overnight. Unfinished tasks and correspondence carry into the next day; most tasks are expected to span several days. Their upfront handling cost is not charged again solely because a day ends. Exact durations remain open.
  • Urgent and flexible tasks. Some tasks have their own deadlines; others only need to finish before the game ends. Late urgent income decreases with growing delay, either daily or in time bands. The exact amounts, intervals and minimum income remain open.
  • You may abandon an accepted case. Handling credits and postage already spent are not refunded. Abandoned cases count toward the same unfinished-case fee at game end.
  • One standard unfinished-case fee. Every accepted case left unfinished at game end, including abandoned cases, incurs the same monetary fee once. The amount is undecided; offers never accepted are exempt.
  • Recipient response times are known. A task states how long the recipient takes to respond; transport and delivery can still delay the return. Exact timing units remain open.
  • Several tasks can run at once. You can work on another case while a reply is travelling. The exact limit is undecided.
  • Price changes are announced. Whenever a tariff-change scenario is used, its date and new charges are visible from the beginning.
  • Your bank pays both directions. Outgoing correspondence and replies count toward your expenses.

What playing could look like

This is the intended flow, not yet a complete turn rule.

  1. Choose a task

    Decide which business opportunity is worth pursuing.

  2. Plan the day secretly

    Everyone selects the day’s full set of actions at the same time.

  3. Reveal and resolve

    Reveal everyone’s full daily plan together, then resolve traffic and delivery publicly.

  4. Bring the reply back

    Keep working on other cases while the exchange is underway.

  5. Complete the business

    Earn the task’s income and account for postal costs.

Example: your bank asks a customer for confirmation needed to complete a business matter. The request reaches the customer through a local post office; a new reply returns to your bank. The message, response rules, timing and reward would be designed for play.

Ideas saved for later

Batching correspondence: several addressed letters could share a private capsule to Jindřišská, where they are sorted for onward destinations. The designer suggests using fixed dispatch costs to make packing and timing strategic. There is no game limit on letters per capsule; exact charging rules remain undecided.

Credit amounts and costs: the budget is chosen as a constraint, but five units remains an example. Tasks consume credits. The full handling cost is paid upfront. Credits reset to the standard budget each morning; unused credits expire. Exact amounts and costs remain open.

Calendar scenario: the designer notes that several days could span 1 March 1937. The research reports messenger charges rising from 1 to 2 Kč in the inner delivery zone and falling from 5 to 2 Kč in the outer zone; the public 40-h pneumatic surcharge stayed unchanged. Exact zones and which service uses each rate still need care. This scenario is an option, not yet a mandatory event. Its date and charges are announced before planning begins.

Price-change experiment: test whether a case started before the rise but completed afterwards incurs a monetary surcharge, potentially making it dearer than starting after the rise. Compare this with charging new prices only for services used after the change. This is a fictional game-cost experiment, not a verified historical unfinished-case penalty. Values and triggers are undecided; do not implement it yet.

Bank workday: explore whether ordinary banking work makes correspondence ready early or late, possibly with replies prepared before new requests. Shared dispatch windows are not chosen. Any reply workload must respect the handling cost already paid upfront. Research prompt: 24 February–3 March 1937.

Service options: prepaid fast replies with a waiting messenger; express delivery; single- and double-pipe operation; a scenario spanning the tariff change.

These options are recorded, not adopted mechanics. Historical availability and prices need period-specific evidence.

The experience we are aiming for

Quick simultaneous choices, simple rules with depth, and several viable ways to succeed. Other players change your opportunities while pursuing their own interests. Setbacks should be recoverable, with room to chat and no need to negotiate trades.

Historical foundation: Jindřišská is the working hub for the documented private-tube model. Exact private access and nine fair bank identities still need resolution. Business missions and rewards are game inventions unless specifically evidenced.
