# First banking transaction: invoice collection

Draft for discussion, 15 November 2026. A specific, fictional game transaction, not a documented historic bank form or verified 1936/37 collection/settlement procedure. The goal is to give the request/reply journey a clear purpose. No exact task rule is adopted by writing this example.

## Card: Inkaso faktury — collection of a customer’s invoice

- **Your role:** a bank acting for a business customer.
- **Business situation:** your customer wants you to arrange collection of an unpaid invoice from a buyer in Smíchov. The buyer and invoice are fictional. The correspondence is routed to the Smíchov delivery area via historic postal office Praha 55, not to a proven tube-connected bank branch or a fictitious direct customer connection.
- **Request:** send the invoice details and a request for payment authorization.
- **Route:** your bank → central office in Jindřišská → Praha 55 → buyer through the final delivery step.
- **Automatic response:** once the request has actually reached the buyer under the delivery rules, the game generates a signed payment authorization. Merely reaching Praha 55 is not receipt by the buyer. Return posting/collection is accounted for in the same explicit timing rule, rather than silently instantaneous.
- **Return:** buyer → Praha 55 → Jindřišská → your bank. These are game-route stages, not a claim that all steps historically occupied an identical interval.
- **Completion:** the authorization arrives at your bank before closing time. Settlement is abstracted as occurring after receipt; do not imply a signature alone historically proves funds cleared. No automatic random buyer default in this first example, and no opponent can withhold the reply.
- **Income:** a service fee, provisionally **5 Kč** for discussion. This is an invented game payout, not a historical fee or a purchasing-power claim. The invoice’s face value belongs to the customer and is not the bank’s profit. No physical cash travels in the capsule.
- **Costs:** subtract the applicable game postal costs. Their exact amounts and treatment of private/public legs remain open. The source’s 40 h per private capsule must not be confused with 40 h per public item or treated as the price of the complete exchange.
- **Commitment:** the case occupies one slot in a limited bank workload until completion or withdrawal. Number of slots, withdrawal consequences and capsule capacity are unresolved. This proposed limit prevents an assumed unlimited free flood of all tasks.
- **Late result:** no fee from this time-limited opportunity; do not add loss of customer principal or missed player turns. Once it can no longer be completed, close the case so it does not permanently occupy capacity. Exact closure timing and any weaker late reward remain for testing.

## The decision to test

Is using limited case/capsule capacity on this Smíchov exchange worthwhile under current traffic, compared with a different available task? Holding several letters for the same direction might save capsule costs, but delay a fee. This trade-off is a proposal, not yet implemented or tested. Capsule loading and movement rules must make it real.

## Limits and next step

This is a draft task card, not a playable full round or a proven balanced offer. No route timing, dispatch priority, final delivery delay, end-of-day round count, exact costs, or net profit has been invented as historical fact. Next define those few timings for one outward-and-return example, then compare it with one nearer job at a different fee. Do not add loans, interest calculations, borrower default, or institution powers before the core loop works.
