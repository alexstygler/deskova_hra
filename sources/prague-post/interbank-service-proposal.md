# Interbank correspondence — candidate for testing

16 November 2026. Designer’s original note:

> banka si může vybrat jestli bude komunikovat s jinou bankou - donutí tu banku vyřizovat jinou věc než to, co jí přidávaá peníze, ty z toho máš nějaké peníze, ale ne moc. spíš uškodíš, že narušíš jednosměrné potrubí a čas na zpracování odpovědi

This is a proposed mechanic, not an adopted change to the interaction brief. The designer invites use if it is good; the assistant recommends exploring a modified useful-service version before adopting forced disruption.

## Assessment

The promising element is correspondence between actual players: a profitable task for one bank creates genuine work for another. As described, however, small sender profit and primarily damaging another player’s time makes it a targeted attack. That conflicts with the earlier preference for actions mainly taken for one's own benefit, avoiding repeated victims. Nine players could overload one bank or decide the winner by whom they target.

Do not assume a bank-to-bank letter occupies a public one-pipe branch: the supported working route is A → Jindřišská → B, using their private connections; private pipe counts remain unresolved. Delaying a bank through a forced response is an invented service obligation, not a historical rule established by either report.

## Modified candidate, not confirmed rules

- A sender has a legitimate, concrete business case needing a confirmation from another bank.
- Sender pays its own task credits upfront and the agreed postage; it cannot silently spend a rival’s credit budget or make it negative.
- Consider a visible small processing fee for the responding bank. A bank that is earning from this response is being offered work, rather than only being punished.
- Recipient effort could affect its public processing queue, with a known response procedure rather than a surprise extra decision after full-day plans are revealed. Requests arriving today could become work visible before tomorrow’s plan. None of this queue timing is adopted.
- Prevent many senders repeatedly burdening one recipient; any per-bank obligation limit would be distinct from the rejected capsule letter-count limit. Exact protection is undecided.
- Postage payer, recipient compensation, timing and any credit allocation must be explicit. Automatic replies were only an earlier proposal; player-controlled service is a distinct variant to compare, not a silent replacement.
- Test whether the modified service has real choices, or merely adds money transfers and bookkeeping. It must fit the 40–50 minute target.

## Next questions

1. Does the designer want some deliberate targeted interference, changing the earlier brief, or disruption only as an incidental result of useful business?
2. Should the responding bank receive a small processing fee?

Do not describe the service as implemented or historically established. Keep the raw idea and the assistant’s recommendation distinct.


## Adopted direction — 16 November 2026

The designer now explicitly prioritizes enjoyment and concrete game rules over
pinpoint historical fidelity, adopting one bidirectional pipe per player bank
as a game simplification. Useful interbank business may incidentally hurt the
other bank, and the replying bank earns a processing fee. The earlier assessment
above remains dated development history: the core service direction and recipient
fee are now chosen, while fee payer, timing, response obligations, recipient
workload/credits and repeat-target protection remain unresolved. No mandatory
credit drain, same-day replan or historically verified private pipe count is
established. Next questions: whether requests become next-morning work and who
funds the responding bank's fee.


## Next-morning handling and fee funding

The designer confirms that requests actually received become known work before
the next morning’s plan. Transport delays still affect arrival. With no user
preference on who pays, use requester-paid recipient fees as a starting rule.
Amount, payment timing, recipient credit usage, mandatory response and direction
control remain open. This updates the earlier pending questions above.


## Response resource and direction decisions

The designer chooses processing time and pipe access only, without extra recipient
credits. Bank-pipe directions follow an automatic schedule. Exact phase count,
priority, processing time and mandatory versus deferrable reply handling remain
open. Player-chosen action order still applies within the eventual timetable.


## Daily phases and reply preparation chosen

One incoming phase followed by one outgoing phase per day is chosen. Interbank
replies must be prepared on the next working day after actual receipt; this does
not guarantee delivery to their original sender that day. The processing-time
constraint, central transfers and overload handling remain open. With unlimited
letters per capsule, pipe access alone may not create meaningful competition;
preparation slots and final processing are next questions, not adopted rules.


## Timing revision under exploration — 17 November 2026

The designer proposes that incoming morning service defers an otherwise early
dispatch to afternoon, potentially reducing same-day reach. The earlier fixed
incoming-then-outgoing schedule therefore needs revision; if every morning is
always incoming, an additional letter does not cause that delay. Conditional
early dispatch and destination cutoff markers are proposals, not selected rules.
Preparation slots remain unchosen. Required replies complete a case automatically
on return; no final processing slot is required.
