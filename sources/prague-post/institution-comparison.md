## What can and cannot be concluded

The roster has visibly different positions. That is sufficient to reject an untested assumption that counting equal moves along a geographically drawn map creates equal starts. It is not sufficient to name the fastest institution, count actual switches, infer private pipe counts, or guarantee that every private line is a physically independent direct link to Jindřišská. A crossing is not necessarily a junction; geographical proximity to a public branch is not proof of access to it. Even a nearer institution may face less favourable destinations or greater congestion.

Page 3 does not supply a complete weighted operating graph. The gameplay has not defined what a movement costs, how a capsule advances, whether intermediate places add delay, when replies appear, or how shared capacity is allocated. Numerical speed rankings or win-rate claims now would be invented.

## Recommended first fair-access prototype (proposal)

Keep institutional locations on the historical illustration. Abstract the private-connection leg: every player hands correspondence into the same game queue at Jindřišská and receives returns through the same timing rule. Do not count private line length as an extra player-specific movement cost. This is a gameplay abstraction, not a historical assertion that all connections had equal length, topology, or speed. It removes one source of starting asymmetry without drawing fictitious historical junctions.

Use equal starting funds, correspondence capacity, processing priority rules, and equivalent opportunity distributions; no institutional special powers yet. Replies are handled by the game, with no owner veto. Inactive institutions can remain destinations so lower player counts do not automatically change available exchanges. None of these proposals is adopted by the user.

Equal hub access guarantees equality only for that abstracted access rule. Other advantages can still arise from opportunity selection, resolution order, and congestion. It also removes some geographical differences from player identities; the wider public network remains shared and playable.

## Alternative if private geography must affect play

Trace the private links and their actual attachment points with uncertainty labels, then define a playable graph. Measure outbound AND return action/time costs to the same weighted destination set, nearest and farthest viable exchanges, bottleneck exposure, and directional waiting. Rewards or task portfolios can then be adjusted to comparable expected net income per effort and risk. Do not add a flat money bonus purely from visual distance, or assume longer-distance rewards automatically fix the difference.

Test at 2, 4, and 9 with comparable opportunities. Rotate participants among institutions and vary demand scenarios. Compare net income, completed exchanges, time until first completed exchange, and unproductive waits, as well as total playtime. A historical reconstruction and a future simulation cannot by themselves establish human-game balance.

## Follow-up: did outgoing private mail go through the central office?

The primary source describes outgoing correspondence placed in a returning private capsule, with the institution arranging for the central office to inspect the returned capsule. List 125 gives an outgoing Anglo-československá banka letter to Argentina with a central-office number; list 126 describes a Národní banka item through the central office and Praha 7; list 128 describes an outgoing Česká banka Union envelope with a label added by the central station. These support central processing as the working model for this documented use of the private tube service. They do not establish a universal legal or operational requirement covering every letter from every institution, every period, or ordinary mail sent without using its tube connection. No verified direct institution-to-institution bypass is established by the consulted passages.

Thus using Jindřišská as the game handoff is historically supported, rather than an arbitrary invented hub. Equal handoff timing for every player remains a gameplay abstraction; a common hub does not prove equal private travel lengths, waiting times, or capacity. Physical independent direct pipes and universal routing should not be inferred from examples alone.
