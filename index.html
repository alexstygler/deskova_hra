Let’s make a board game.

A shared record of our conclusions, experiments, and next steps.

Current focus

Describe one game mechanic you enjoy and why it feels fun. A memorable moment from a game is enough.

Next: Identify the experience we want to borrow before proposing mechanics.

Agreed goals

  • Support approximately 2–9 players.
  • Keep the game enjoyable at nine players, with little frustrating waiting.
  • Target no more than about 25% longer playtime at nine players compared with 2–4 players. The exact comparison and session length remain to be defined.

How we will work

  • Explore one small topic at a time.
  • The assistant maintains this notebook and records each conclusion.
  • After finishing a topic, briefly state what was decided and the next focus.
  • Keep confirmed decisions separate from tentative ideas. Revisit decisions when playtests give us a reason.
  • Use this HTML notebook for the project; later add a playable web demo and visual design experiments.

Ideas to explore — not decisions

Simultaneous choices, fixed rounds, shared events, small-group interaction, and timed negotiation are possible ways to reduce waiting. None has been chosen.

Theme, core mechanics, victory conditions, complexity, and game length are still open.

Roadmap — for reference

  1. Explore favorite mechanics and the experiences behind them.
  2. Develop a few candidate concepts.
  3. Explore theme and mechanics together.
  4. Prototype and test pacing at two, four, and nine players.
  5. Refine rules, then develop visual designs.

This is a tentative sequence, not a checklist to tackle all at once.
